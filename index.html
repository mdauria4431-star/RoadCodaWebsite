One day, start to finish

Nobody retypes anything. Every line below comes from something that already happened.

  • The board

    Today's loads and stops, who's on them, what's running late and why — with the reason recorded at the time instead of reconstructed on Friday. Route planner puts the stops in the right order for the windows, the weight and the driver's hours.

  • The driver's phone

    Arrive, scan, photograph, get the signature, depart. Three taps a stop, works with no signal, sends when it comes back. Barcode scanning with the phone camera or a Bluetooth scanner, returns and empties, trip sheets for paper drivers.

  • What your customer sees

    Emails when the truck is on the way, running late or delivered. A tracking link that expires after the stop. A portal with their loads, documents and invoices. Delivery ratings, so you hear from an unhappy store before your competitor does.

  • When something goes wrong

    A short or damaged pallet recorded at the stop, onto the to-follow list, acknowledged by the driver. OS&D, refusals and closed stores with codes, not phone calls.

  • Billing

    Invoices drafted when the load closes, priced off the contract, with tolls, detention past free time, stop charges and accessorials already on. Weekly master invoices or per load, your own numbering, customer activity files, QuickBooks export, and toll statements reconciled against what you billed.

  • Driver pay

    By mile, hour, stop, load or salary, with detention and extras. Drivers see their own week in the app. Files for ADP, Paychex, Paylocity or Gusto, plus office staff payroll with a time clock.

  • Safety and compliance

    Accident and injury reports taken at the scene with photos and witness statements. OSHA 300, 300A and 301 logs. Incidents per million miles by driver, truck, customer and location. Driver files, CDLs, medical cards, MVRs and inspections with due dates. Claims and subrogation. Handbooks signed on the phone.

  • The numbers

    Profit by customer, route, truck and driver — revenue against driver pay, fuel, tolls and costs. IFTA miles and fuel by state each quarter. Every report exports.

The things people ask

We already have a system.

Most systems dispatch loads and raise invoices, and stop there. Driver files, medical cards and MVRs are a separate subscription — usually $5 to $12 a driver a month. Injury logs and OSHA records are separate again, or paper. Delivery notices and tracking links for your customers belong to a different category of software altogether, at a few thousand a month. RoadCoda does all of it, on one bill.

Two questions worth asking your current vendor: what does a second user cost, and how long does it take to prove a delivery from three weeks ago?

My drivers won't use it.

Three taps a stop, on the phone they already carry. No app store, no training day. If a driver can send a photo to his wife, he can run a route on RoadCoda.

How long before we're running on it?

One route in a week, alongside whatever you do now. Everything in two to six weeks. Your customers, stores, drivers, trucks and rates get loaded for you.

What happens to our data if we leave?

It's yours. One button exports the lot — loads, stops, photos, invoices, driver pay, safety records. We keep it for 60 days after you go, then delete it.

Who answers the phone?

The person who wrote the software. RoadCoda is one person, and you should know that before you buy rather than after. What you get for it is a direct line instead of a ticket number.
